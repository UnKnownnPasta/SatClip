# VHM: Versatile and Honest Vision Language Model for Remote Sensing Image Analysis

## Problem
RS VLMs were trained only on factual questions about objects that exist in the image, so they happily answer nonsense, for example giving the colour of an object that is not there. Their captions also covered only a few prominent objects, limiting what the model learns about the whole scene.

## Approach
VHM follows the LLaVA recipe: CLIP-Large at 336 px, Vicuna-v1.5 7B, with a multi-level visual representation and a compression layer. Training is two-stage: pretraining all components on VersaD, then supervised fine-tuning on three instruction sets (VersaD-Instruct, VariousRS-Instruct and the honesty set HnstD). HnstD pairs factual questions with deceptive ones (about non-existent objects, or about colour in panchromatic images) so the model learns to say the premise is false.

## Data and benchmarks
- VersaD: about 1.4M RS images (0.08 to 153 m per pixel) with rich multi-sentence captions generated by Gemini-Vision; a sampled accuracy check put caption accuracy at 82.3%.
- HnstD: over 45k question-answer pairs, manually checked, across presence, colour, absolute position and relative position.
- Evaluation: scene classification, VQA, visual grounding, plus new tasks (counting, image modality, image resolution estimation, geometric measurement, building vectorizing, multi-label classification, honest QA).

## Key results
From the paper's tables:
- Honest QA (HnstD): VHM scores 85.06 on presence; colour 81.50 factual, 93.33 and 93.00 on the two deceptive types; absolute position 76.79 factual, 90.67 deceptive; relative position 47.52 factual, 87.67 deceptive. Qwen-VL-Chat scores only 8.33 on deceptive colour questions about absent objects.
- Image modality recognition 95% accuracy; image resolution estimation mean absolute error 0.24; building vectorizing complexity-aware IoU 71.25%.
- Pretraining on VersaD beat SkyScript pretraining by over 43% on grounding despite SkyScript's higher caption accuracy, suggesting richer captions matter more than perfect ones.

## Limitations
The authors state VHM cannot do pixel-wise perception, so no segmentation or change detection. Optical RGB only, single date, 7B. Honesty is limited to four question templates; relative position factual accuracy (47.52) is much lower than its deceptive accuracy (87.67), which suggests some answers become refusals at the cost of correct factual answers (our reading, not the authors' claim). Honesty is a binary learned behaviour, not a calibrated probability. Captions are themselves model generated (about 18% inaccurate by their own check).

## What it means for SatClip
- This is the nearest RS prior art for "the assistant says no when the premise is wrong". Cite it honestly rather than claiming no RS assistant refuses.
- The difference to stress: VHM refuses based on what the language model believes it sees; SatClip abstains based on instrument quality signals (cloud fraction, SAR noise floor, mask size, temporal gap) with a calibrated threshold. VHM gives no confidence score, no scene IDs, dates or masks.
- Adopt the HnstD idea for SatClip's parser tests: include deceptive questions ("how much rice is flooded in a district with no rice", "NDVI on a date with no Sentinel-2 pass") and require the system to correct the premise.
- Watch the factual-versus-deceptive gap: measure both refusal correctness and false refusals so SatClip's abstention does not become over-cautious.
