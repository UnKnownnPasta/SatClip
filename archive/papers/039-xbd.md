# xBD: A Dataset for Assessing Building Damage from Satellite Imagery

## Problem
After a disaster, responders need building-level damage estimates within roughly a day or two, which in-person surveys cannot deliver safely. Machine learning needed a large, consistently labelled pre/post-event dataset and a damage scale agreed with responders.

## Approach
The authors worked with disaster response agencies (including NASA, CAL FIRE, FEMA and the California Air National Guard) to define a four-level Joint Damage Scale: no damage, minor damage, major damage, destroyed. They annotated building polygons on pre- and post-event imagery with these ordinal labels, plus environmental factors such as fire, water and smoke. A baseline uses a U-Net for building localization followed by a classifier for damage level.

## Data and benchmarks
19 disaster events (hurricanes, floods, wildfires, earthquakes, tsunamis, tornadoes, volcanic eruptions) from the Maxar/DigitalGlobe Open Data Program, RGB imagery below 0.8 m GSD, 22,068 images, 45,362 square km and 850,736 building polygons. It was the basis of the xView2 challenge.

## Key results
From the paper (ar5iv version): baseline localization IoU of 0.66 for buildings (0.97 for background); damage classification weighted F1 of only 0.2654, ranging from about 0.009 for major damage to about 0.66 for no damage.

## Limitations
Labels and disaster types are highly unbalanced, damage cues between adjacent levels are subtle, and generalising the scale across disaster types adds label noise (all noted by the authors). Commercial sub-metre RGB imagery only, so nothing directly reusable at Sentinel resolution. Few events in South Asia. ## What it means for SatClip
- Per-building damage grading is not something SatClip can measure from 10 m Sentinel-2 or Sentinel-1; the evidence card should explicitly abstain on "was this house damaged" and offer area-level proxies instead (flooded extent, burned area via dNBR, SAR coherence or backscatter change over a settlement).
- Adopt its pre/post-event framing and an ordinal severity vocabulary for explanations, but derive severity from measured area fractions, not from a model guess.
- Its responder-co-designed scale is a good model for co-designing SatClip's confidence wording with district disaster officials.
- Do not use xBD for calibration; resolution and licence do not match.
