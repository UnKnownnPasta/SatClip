# Geo-R1: Improving Few-Shot Geospatial Referring Expression Understanding with Reinforcement Fine-Tuning

## Problem
Referring expression tasks in RS (find "the second ship from the left near the dock") need costly expert labels; VRSBench alone needed about 1,000 hours of label checking. Supervised fine-tuning with only a handful of examples overfits and generalizes poorly.

## Approach
Geo-R1 post-trains Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct with GRPO (and DAPO in places), using the VLM-R1 and EasyR1 codebases. The model must write a reasoning chain first and then output boxes. Rewards are rule based, following DeepSeek-R1: a task-agnostic format reward plus a task-specific metric reward (box overlap metrics for grounding and detection; for segmentation a mask GIoU computed after prompting a frozen SAM with the predicted boxes, which makes a non-differentiable pipeline trainable). The exact per-task reward formulas were not checked in detail for this entry. Settings are 1, 5 and 10 shots per category.

## Data and benchmarks
Few-shot versions of VRSBench (referring comprehension), NWPU-VHR-10 (open-vocabulary detection, four classes) and EarthReason (generalized referring segmentation); cross-dataset zero-shot tests on DIOR-RSVG and RRSIS-D; plus a SAR referring set built from SARDet (six classes, 10 shots). Released checkpoints are on Hugging Face; annotations only, images from original sources.

## Key results
From the paper's tables:
- VRSBench 10-shot: GRPO beats SFT by 12.30 points Acc@0.5 using 260 samples (0.71% of the full set), and surpasses all full-data baselines except Qwen2.5-VL itself.
- The GRPO advantage shrinks with more data: 13.03 points at 1 shot down to 0.47 at 200 shots, converging near the full-data SFT result (62.91%).
- Few-shot detection: SFT often falls below the zero-shot baseline; GRPO's lead is 4.72 mAP at 10 shots and 6.58 at 5 shots.
- Segmentation: with 240 samples Geo-R1 reaches about 83% of a fully trained SegEarth-R1.
- SAR referring (10-shot): mean Acc@0.5 rises from 15.31 (base) and 14.87 (SFT) to 25.00, with ship 56.0 but bridge 5.0 and tank 7.1.

## Limitations
Gains are largest in the very-low-data regime and vanish with ample labels. Absolute SAR accuracy remains low. Reasoning chains are not checked for faithfulness, only the final box is rewarded. Evaluation is high-resolution object grounding, not land-cover extent or change. No confidence, abstention or provenance outputs. The author list differs slightly between the arXiv and journal records.

## What it means for SatClip
- Adopt the training recipe if SatClip ever fine-tunes its small parser VLM: a verifiable reward (does the parsed district, date range and instrument match the gold query?) with GRPO needs only tens of Indian examples, which suits a hackathon budget.
- The SAR table is a useful quote for judges: an RL-tuned RS VLM still finds most SAR targets wrongly at 10 shots, which supports measuring flood water with a backscatter threshold rather than a model.
- Idea to borrow: reward only what can be checked. SatClip's evidence card is effectively the same philosophy applied at inference time; every claim is checkable against scene metadata.
- Watch out for "reason first" chains being presented as explanations; Geo-R1 does not verify them, so SatClip should keep explanations tied to card fields.
