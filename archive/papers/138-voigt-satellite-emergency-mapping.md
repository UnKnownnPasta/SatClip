# Voigt et al. 2016, global trends in satellite emergency mapping

## Problem
Satellite Earth observation has been used for rapid disaster assessment for years through mechanisms such as the International Charter Space and Major Disasters, Copernicus Emergency Management Service and Sentinel Asia. There was no global synthesis of where, when and how such emergency mapping was actually deployed.

## Approach
- Compiles records of satellite rapid response and emergency mapping activations from major international and regional services between 2000 and 2014.
- Analyses spatial patterns and temporal trends, and compares them with the distribution of natural disasters.
- Discusses the outlook for Earth observation in disaster response and mitigation.

## Data and benchmarks
More than 1000 disaster incidents in which satellite monitoring was used, 2000 to 2014. No model benchmark.

## Key results
- Use of satellite emergency mapping grew over the 15 years reviewed.
- Activity is most intense in Asia and Europe, and broadly follows the geographic, physical and temporal distribution of natural disasters.
- Per-hazard and per-region figures from the full paper were not verified for this entry.

## Limitations
- Counts activations and products, not whether responders used them or whether decisions improved.
- Ends in 2014, before free Sentinel-1/2 data became routine and before cloud platforms and AI assistants.
- Multi-agency authorship; data coverage depends on what each service records.

## What it means for SatClip
- Indian disasters (floods especially) are well within the footprint of existing emergency mapping, so SatClip should cite and link official products (for example NRSC, Charter or Copernicus EMS maps) when they exist instead of competing with them.
- The gap SatClip addresses is delivery to non-experts: emergency maps are expert products, while district officials and journalists need a plain-language number with sources and limits. Pair this with entry 069 on humanitarian uptake.
- Because the paper measures activations, not impact, SatClip should log whether its cards were used and whether abstentions were respected, to build the uptake evidence this literature lacks.
