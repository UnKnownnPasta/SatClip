# Problem evidence brief: who needs answers from satellite imagery, and why they struggle today

Prepared for SatClip (SIH26167). All sources were fetched and checked on 2026-10-04 unless marked otherwise. Each claim is paraphrased; short quoted fragments are under 15 words. Items marked **[UNVERIFIED]** or **[GAP]** are honest notes on what we could not confirm.

Legend: (accessed 2026-10-04) applies to every link below.

---

## 1. Who needs answers, and what they do today

### Disaster management (NRSC, NDEM, DDMAs)

1. NRSC's Decision Support Centre monitors floods, cyclones, drought, landslides, earthquakes and forest fires using space and aerial inputs, and runs NDEM as the national GIS repository with decision support tools for state and central disaster authorities. [NRSC DMS overview](https://www.nrsc.gov.in/nrscnew/Apps_DMS_overview.php) (accessed 2026-10-04)
2. India told UN COPUOS that about 300 flood inundation maps and value-added products were sent to disaster management departments in 2024, a centrally produced output for a country with over 700 districts. [India statement, COPUOS STSC 2025](https://www.unoosa.org/documents/pdf/copuos/stsc/2025/Statements/6_India.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-04)
3. NRSC flood maps reach state governments and MHA through the NDEM portal and Bhuvan, combining SAR (Sentinel-1A, Radarsat-2, ALOS-2) with optical AWiFS; the UP 2022 report mapped 21 districts but states no delivery time. [NRSC UP flood report](https://www.nrsc.gov.in/sites/default/files/pdf/DMSP/SatellitebasedAnalysis-FloodMappingMonitoringinUP.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-04)
4. Dissemination to Relief Commissioners and District Magistrates historically ran over VSAT, FTP, web and e-mail; during the Kosi breach, updates came roughly every 3 to 4 days. [NIDM and UN-OOSA workshop slides on the DSS](https://un-spider.org/sites/default/files/Presentations%20NIDM-UNOOSA%20workshop%20-Decision%20Support%20System.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-04)
5. Example of turnaround: an NRSC Assam flood map from a Resourcesat-2A AWiFS pass on 2 June 2024 (11:00) carries an issue date of 4 June 2024, two days later. [NDEM Assam flood map, 02-06-2024](https://ndem.nrsc.gov.in/documents/Disaster_Document/2024/AS/asflood50dsc02062024/asflood50dsc02062024_vap1.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-04)
6. Counter-example: another Assam map from a 26 June 2024 pass was issued the same day, so turnaround varies from same day to days. These maps are labelled preliminary, for official agencies, with no ground verification. [NDEM Assam flood map, 26-06-2024](https://ndem.nrsc.gov.in/documents/Disaster_Document/2024/AS/asflood50dsc26062024_1100hrs/asflood50dsc26062024_1100hrs_vap1.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-04)
7. NDEM v5.0 (for MHA and its emergency control room) and Bhuvan Panchayat v4.0 were launched on 28 June 2024; NDEM has run since 2013. [OpenGov Asia](https://archive.opengovasia.com/2024/06/27/india-pioneering-geoportals-for-governance-and-disaster-management/) (accessed 2026-10-04)
8. In the 2018 Kerala floods ISRO activated the International Charter; NRSC produced four flood products from ResourceSat-2, Sentinel-1, RADARSAT-2 and ALOS-2 covering 14 districts. [International Charter activation 582](https://disasterscharter.org/ru/web/guest/activations/-/article/flood-in-india-activation-582-) (accessed 2026-10-04)
9. An NIDM review of SDMA and DDMA staffing found 182 of 542 sanctioned SDMA posts vacant, and noted unclear professional roles and no capacity building framework. It does not address GIS skills specifically. [NIDM, Status of HR at SDMAs and DDMAs](https://nidm.gov.in/pdf/acrc26/Parallel%20Session%20I/ED%20RinadequacySDMA&DDMA.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-04)
10. A 2010 UN-SPIDER mission to India recommended state-level policy action for capacity development and access to space-based information for disaster agencies. [UN-SPIDER Institutional Strengthening Mission](https://un-spider.org/es/node/8714) (accessed 2026-10-04)

**[GAP]** We found no official, published service-level figure (for example "map within N hours of acquisition") for NRSC flood products, and no survey of DDMA officials' GIS skills. Turnaround evidence above is anecdotal from individual map sheets.

### Agriculture and crop insurance (PMFBY, YES-TECH)

11. The Standing Committee on Agriculture (August 2021) called claim delays one of the biggest PMFBY challenges and noted crop cutting experiments are time consuming and labour intensive. [PRS summary of the Committee report](https://prsindia.org/files/policy/policy_committee_reports/Report%20summary_PMFBY.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-04)
12. The same Committee recommended that all states adopt smart sampling using satellite data or drones in place of traditional crop cutting. [PRS summary](https://prsindia.org/files/policy/policy_committee_reports/Report%20summary_PMFBY.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-04)
13. The Agriculture Minister told the Lok Sabha (August 2023) that Rs 2,761.10 crore in claims were pending up to 2021-22, citing delayed yield data transmission and yield disputes among the reasons. [Orissa Post](https://www.orissapost.com/govt-says-crop-insurance-claims-worth-rs-2761-10-crore-pending-under-pmfby-till-2021-22/amp/) (accessed 2026-10-04)
14. MNCFC pilots using satellite data, drones and AI/ML suggested crop cutting experiments could be cut by 30 to 70 percent; smart sampling covered 96 districts in 9 states in Kharif 2019. [ReliefWeb, PIB reply](https://reliefweb.int/report/india/use-modern-technology-assessing-damage-crops) (accessed 2026-10-04)
15. YES-TECH started in Kharif 2023 for paddy and wheat, giving satellite-based yield a minimum 30 percent weight alongside crop cutting experiments. [Lok Sabha Unstarred Q 1213, 11 Feb 2025](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/2988517/1/AU1213_TGEWQ3.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-04)
16. Madhya Pradesh used YES-TECH at full weight for some crops from Kharif 2023, but soybean farmers in Sehore, Vidisha and Bhopal disputed payouts, and experts called for independent validation. [Down To Earth, May 2026](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/amp/story/agriculture/can-technology-make-indias-crop-insurance-payouts-more-accurate) (accessed 2026-10-04)

**[GAP]** We did not find hard numbers on how long agriculture extension officers wait for remote sensing outputs, or on their GIS training levels.

### Urban planners

17. NITI Aayog (September 2021) found 65 percent of India's 7,933 urban settlements have no master plan, and planning posts are vacant up to 42 percent in some states; it proposed 8,268 lateral entry town planner posts. [PRS summary of NITI Aayog report](https://prsindia.org/policy/report-summaries/reforms-in-urban-planning-capacity-in-india) (accessed 2026-10-04)

### Journalists and fact-checkers

18. GIJN's guide for reporters recommends free Sentinel-2 and Landsat via EO Browser for regional change, but notes Google Earth Engine needs JavaScript or Python skills. [GIJN, Sept 2023](https://gijn.org/resource/resources-for-finding-and-using-satellite-images/) (accessed 2026-10-04)
19. BOOM's May 2025 report on Operation Sindoor misinformation logged 13 AI-related fact-checks out of 101, including AI-generated images, showing how much verification work now involves synthetic visuals. [BOOM Live](https://www.boomlive.in/amp/boom-research/boom-misinformation-report-may-2025-operation-sindoor-india-pakistan-armed-conflict-28733) (accessed 2026-10-04)

**[GAP]** We found no survey of how many Indian newsrooms can analyse satellite imagery in house.

### Researchers and public-health users

20. A PLOS ONE study (2025) on Western Ghats districts argued Sentinel-1 SAR is better for tracking water-related disease risk, because it sees small water bodies and works through monsoon cloud. [NERC Open Research Archive](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/538849/) (accessed 2026-10-04)

## 2. The skills gap is real

21. India's National Task Force on Geospatial Education (2013) estimated only about 800 to 1,200 degree-holding geospatial professionals, against a need for 5,000 to 8,000 more by 2015. [MoE, Task Force report](https://education.gov.in/sites/upload_files/mhrd/files/RportNTFGE2013.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-04)
22. The same report estimated 25,000 to 50,000 trained users but a need for about 500,000 more, at 50,000 to 100,000 a year. [MoE, Task Force report](https://education.gov.in/sites/upload_files/mhrd/files/RportNTFGE2013.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-04)
23. The task force said many central and state government professionals and decision-makers need basic geospatial orientation. [MoE, Task Force report](https://education.gov.in/sites/upload_files/mhrd/files/RportNTFGE2013.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-04)
24. A DST presentation (GSI 2025) projects about 10 lakh geospatial jobs by 2030 and says NGP 2022 calls for long-term capacity development, with geospatial education from school onwards. [DST NGP capacity building slides](https://geospatialworld.net/gsi/2025/presentations/capacity-building/day1/session1/geospatial-capacity-building-program-initiative-ngp-dst-gsi2025-dr_manoranjan_mohanty.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-04)
25. DST says NGP 2022 democratised access to geospatial data and that skilled manpower is essential for a self-reliant ecosystem. [DST](https://dst.gov.in/national-geospatial-policy-2022-marked-landmark-reform-democratized-access-geospatial-data) (accessed 2026-10-04)
26. An industry talk at GSI 2025 cites an "acute shortage" of high calibre geospatial professionals and entry-level gaps in domain and IT knowledge. [Mathur, GSI 2025](https://geospatialworld.net/gsi/2025/presentations/capacity-building/day1/session1/future-ready-talent-policy-pathways-changing-world-gsi2025-rajesh_c_mathur.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-04)
27. ISRO's IIRS outreach programme reached over 1,000 networked institutions and lists more than 1.2 lakh benefited participants, showing large training effort. Its yearly cumulative figures are internally inconsistent, so use the headline number with care. [ISRO at CEOS WGCapD-9, 2020](https://ceos.org/document_management/Working_Groups/WGCapD/Meetings/WGCapD-9-2020/WGCapD9_1.2_Innovation_Chauhan_ISRO_v1.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-04)
28. A UK report for Copernicus user uptake (2021) frames skills gaps as a primary barrier and aims to make public staff "intelligent customers" of Earth observation. [JNCC Report 690](https://jncc.gov.uk/resources/513a6bfb-b1a3-4809-b6be-2aa464ccb9c9) (accessed 2026-10-04)
29. NRSC researchers themselves propose a multilingual geospatial chatbot over Bhuvan and Sentinel data for policymakers and the public who lack GIS expertise, citing India's 22 official languages. [Saxena and Rao, ISPRS Annals 2025](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/X-5-W2-2025/565/2025/isprs-annals-X-5-W2-2025-565-2025.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-04)

**[GAP]** No recent (post-2022) official count of the geospatial workforce supply in India was found; the 2013 figures are the latest primary numbers.

## 3. Monsoon cloud cover and SAR as the fallback

30. Using MODIS, Shang et al. (2018) found cloud cover above 60 percent over most of India during the June to September monsoon, with only Tamil Nadu and northern India below 40 percent. [Remote Sensing 10(1):122](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/10/1/122) (accessed 2026-10-04)
31. In three Western Ghats districts, July to August cloud cover was 92 percent (2017) and 90 percent (2018), leaving 25 and 23 percent of surface water unmapped by the optical JRC product. [Uday et al., PLOS ONE 2025 (NORA)](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/538849/) (accessed 2026-10-04)
32. NRSC prefers radar for flood mapping because it penetrates cloud and acquires day and night. [NRSC UP flood report](https://www.nrsc.gov.in/sites/default/files/pdf/DMSP/SatellitebasedAnalysis-FloodMappingMonitoringinUP.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-04)
33. A Kerala 2018 and 2019 flood study used Sentinel-1A because heavy rainfall clouds interrupt optical acquisition. [Dhanabalan et al., ISPRS Archives 2021](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLIV-M-3-2021/37/2021/) (accessed 2026-10-04)
34. Note that many NRSC Assam flood maps in June 2024 still used optical AWiFS, which only works where cloud breaks. [NDEM Assam map](https://ndem.nrsc.gov.in/documents/Disaster_Document/2024/AS/asflood50dsc02062024/asflood50dsc02062024_vap1.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-04)

**[UNVERIFIED]** A WION article (July 2026) headline says INSAT-3DS showed clouds over 95 percent of India; the fetched text did not contain the 95 percent figure. [WION](https://www.wionews.com/india-news/isro-satellite-captures-monsoon-clouds-blanketing-95-of-india-imd-issues-rain-alert-1784731019499) (accessed 2026-10-04). Do not cite the number.

## 4. Fluent AI answers on imagery are not trustworthy by default

35. MIT's benchmark of GPT-4V on Earth observation found strong captioning but object localisation of only 0.16 mean IoU (versus 0.68 for specialist models). [Zhang and Wang, arXiv 2401.17600](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.17600v1) (accessed 2026-10-04)
36. The same study found GPT-4V systematically failed at building damage from before and after images (R squared 0.10) and at counting trees (0.20) and animals (0.08). [arXiv 2401.17600](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.17600v1) (accessed 2026-10-04)
37. DisasterM3 tested 14 vision-language models and found state-of-the-art models struggle with disaster tasks, citing the optical to SAR sensor gap and poor damage counting. [Wang et al., arXiv 2505.21089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21089) (accessed 2026-10-04)
38. MIT Lincoln Lab and MIT researchers measured hallucinated proper nouns in LLM-generated remote sensing captions and reduced them by grounding with OpenStreetMap data, showing grounding in external records lowers hallucination. [Anderson et al., arXiv 2501.14905](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14905v1) (accessed 2026-10-04)
39. A UNOOSA policy brief (September 2025) warns that opaque AI outputs cannot be fully trusted, and calls for provenance, robust metadata and audit trails for AI-derived satellite products. [UNOOSA, Ensuring Responsible AI in Space and EO](https://www.unoosa.org/res/oosadoc/data/documents/2025/p/-_0_html/UNOOSA-Ensuring-Responsible-AI-in-Space-and-Earth-Observation.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-04)
40. Even official human-made NRSC maps carry caveats (preliminary, no ground verification, may include rainwater in low-lying areas), which shows disaster users already expect stated limits. [NDEM Assam map 26-06-2024](https://ndem.nrsc.gov.in/documents/Disaster_Document/2024/AS/asflood50dsc26062024_1100hrs/asflood50dsc26062024_1100hrs_vap1.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-04)

**[GAP]** We found no India-specific incident where an AI imagery answer misled a disaster response; the risk is argued from benchmarks, not from documented field harm.

## 5. Data availability

41. Copernicus Sentinel data are available on a free, full and open basis through the Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem; downloading needs a free registered account. [CDSE terms](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/node/10) (accessed 2026-10-04)
42. Sentinel-1C launched on 5 December 2024 on Vega-C to restore a two-satellite SAR constellation after Sentinel-1B failed in August 2022. [UN-SPIDER](https://www.un-spider.org/news-and-events/news/esa-successfully-launches-copernicus-sentinel-1c-satellite) (accessed 2026-10-04)
43. Update: Sentinel-1D launched in November 2025; from 24 June 2026 Sentinel-1C and 1D form the constellation with a 6-day revisit, and Sentinel-1A operations were to end on 29 June 2026. [CDSE news, 28 May 2026](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/node/3949) (accessed 2026-10-04)
44. Sentinel-2 offers a 5-day revisit at the equator with two satellites, 10 m resolution in visible and near infrared, and 13 bands. [ESA Sentinel-2 facts](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/Copernicus/Sentinel-2/Facts_and_figures) (accessed 2026-10-04)
45. CDSE runs a STAC catalogue at stac.dataspace.copernicus.eu/v1 covering Sentinel-1, 2, 3, 5P and 6 plus DEM and land service products; the older endpoint was deprecated from 17 November 2025. [CDSE STAC docs](https://documentation.dataspace.copernicus.eu/APIs/STAC.html) (accessed 2026-10-04)
46. Element 84's Earth Search is a free STAC API over open data on AWS, including the full Sentinel-2 and Landsat Collection 2 catalogues plus Sentinel-1 and Copernicus DEM. [Element 84](https://element84.com/earth-search/) (accessed 2026-10-04)
47. Microsoft Planetary Computer exposes a public STAC API v1.0.0 with search over its hosted Earth science datasets. [Planetary Computer STAC root](https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/api/stac/v1) (accessed 2026-10-04)
48. Google Earth Engine is free for verified noncommercial projects (150 EECU-hours a month at the Community tier), but higher tiers need a billing account or an application, and coding skill is assumed. [Earth Engine noncommercial tiers](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/guides/noncommercial_tiers) (accessed 2026-10-04)

## 6. Competitive landscape (natural language or no-code imagery tools)

49. Google Earth AI with Gemini "geospatial reasoning" (October 2025) is offered to Google Earth Professional users and AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in the US, plus Cloud trusted testers; the post names no India availability or price. [Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/research/new-updates-and-more-access-to-google-earth-ai/) (accessed 2026-10-04)
50. NASA and Microsoft's Earth Copilot answers plain-language questions about NASA data, but was available to NASA scientists and researchers for testing at announcement. [Microsoft blog, Nov 2024](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2024/11/14/from-questions-to-discoveries-nasas-new-earth-copilot-brings-microsoft-ai-capabilities-to-democratize-access-to-complex-data/) (accessed 2026-10-04)
51. Esri's ArcGIS AI assistants (June 2026) cover surveys, business analysis, metadata, code and ArcGIS Pro tasks; several are beta, all sit inside paid ArcGIS user types, and none is a plain-language SAR or optical query tool for non-GIS users. [ArcGIS Blog](https://www.esri.com/arcgis-blog/products/arcgis-online/geoai/whats-new-in-ai-assistants-june-2026), [ArcGIS Online pricing page](https://www.esri.com/en-us/arcgis/products/arcgis-online/pricing) (accessed 2026-10-04)
52. Planet demoed a natural-language Earth data interface with Microsoft's AI for Good Lab in 2023 but said it would not be available soon. [Geo Week News, April 2023](https://www.geoweeknews.com/articles/planet-demos-next-gen-ai-for-satellites/) (accessed 2026-10-04)
53. Microsoft Planetary Computer Pro is a metered Azure service (compute, operations, storage, egress) aimed at enterprises; the pricing page lists no natural-language features. [Azure pricing](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/pricing/details/planetary-computer-pro/) (accessed 2026-10-04)
54. Picterra is a no-code ML platform for detecting objects in satellite, drone and aerial imagery; pricing is on request with no listed free tier. [Software Advice profile](https://www.softwareadvice.com/cafm/picterra-profile/) (accessed 2026-10-04)
55. EarthDaily's agriculture platform sells vegetation, yield and weather APIs to agri-business, lenders and insurers; pricing is not public. [EarthDaily](https://earthdaily.com/platform-api) (accessed 2026-10-04)
56. Within India, NRSC's own chatbot framework over Bhuvan is a research proposal, not a deployed public service, per the paper. [ISPRS Annals 2025](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/X-5-W2-2025/565/2025/isprs-annals-X-5-W2-2025-565-2025.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-04)

**[UNVERIFIED]** Exact ArcGIS Online user type prices were not shown on the fetched Esri pages.

## Summary of gaps

- No published NRSC service-level time for flood maps; only individual map sheets (same day to two days).
- No survey of DDMA, extension officer, or newsroom GIS skills in India.
- Latest primary workforce numbers are from 2013.
- No documented Indian case of AI imagery hallucination harming a response.
